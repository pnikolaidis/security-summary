First up, Zscaler. NVD published four new Client Connector vulnerabilities today, and two of them are critical: an authentication bypass between the client and its own portal, and an unauthenticated remote code execution bug in the connector itself. Next, a maximum severity story. CISA added an Oracle WebLogic flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, and it's about as bad as it gets — a perfect ten out of ten on the severity scale, letting an unauthenticated attacker reach critical data on unpatched Oracle HTTP Server and WebLogic deployments. This isn't theoretical. CISA is citing active, in-the-wild exploitation, and both major trade outlets covering it are treating it as a drop-everything patch situation rather than a routine advisory. If you have any customer running Oracle WebLogic, this is an emergency patch conversation, and it's a natural opener for a broader external attack-surface assessment or pentest engagement — especially for any account that's been putting off a full perimeter review.

Following on identity and email, researchers are tracking a phishing kit called Mirage Two FA that's now hit around four thousand five hundred companies across the US and Europe. It's a commercial phishing-as-a-service toolkit that abuses legitimate Microsoft 365 login flows to sit between the user and Microsoft, relaying session tokens and defeating standard two-factor authentication. Nearly half of targeted mailboxes were potentially compromised. If a customer's MFA story is just whatever Microsoft ships by default, this is your opening to pitch a phishing-resistant MFA or conditional-access review.

Here's an interesting one for anyone selling against managed detection and response providers. ReliaQuest, itself a cybersecurity company, confirmed that hackers impersonating a member of its own security team social-engineered an employee and got into an internal dashboard, as part of the broader ShinyHunters extortion campaign. It's the same trusted-team impersonation playbook behind a lot of the Salesforce and Salesloft-adjacent breaches this year, so it's also a good excuse to check in with anyone still cleaning up from that wave. If you compete with ReliaQuest in the MDR space, this is a legitimate reason to ask a prospect how their current provider handles insider social engineering.
