Good morning. Allie here with your Monday channel brief for August third — five things worth raising on calls today, starting with an active MSP tooling attack and closing with a travel-risk story that touches almost every account you carry.

First up, N-able. Attackers exploited an authentication bypass in N-central, the vendor's remote monitoring and management platform, and got remote administrative access to servers that manage downstream customer systems. Here's the part that raises the urgency: N-able's first attempted fix didn't actually close the hole, and the intrusion continued. The vendor shipped a real fix yesterday, August second. If you're carrying any customer that runs N-central, this is today's call, not this week's. Ask them directly whether they're on the fixed build yet. This is a strong pentest opener — validating exposure on any internet-facing N-central instance — and it's also a natural advisory conversation about the security posture of the RMM tooling itself, since a compromised management platform is a foothold into everything it touches.

Next, Brinks Home. Yes, a physical security brand just confirmed its own data breach, with hackers leaking stolen files. The company says alarm monitoring and core functionality weren't affected, but the breach itself is real and already circulating in trade press. This one's a gift of an opener, because it's memorable without being alarmist — a security company getting breached is exactly the kind of story that makes a vendor-risk or third-party-risk conversation land naturally. Good advisory angle here: a third-party risk assessment, or a look at identity and access controls around vendor-facing systems.

Third, SonicWall. Recently disclosed vulnerabilities in the SMA1000 secure access appliances are being actively chained by the INC ransomware gang for root access and lateral movement. That means a remote-access gateway is being turned into a beachhead for full ransomware intrusions. Any SonicWall account running SMA1000 gear needs a same-day patch confirmation. This is a pentest and advisory two-for-one: an external attack-surface check on the appliances, plus a patch-validation review. And if the account's been sitting on legacy SMA1000 hardware for a while, this is your opening to talk about moving them to a modern SASE edge instead of just patching and waiting for the next one.

Fourth, some good news you can use as a warm call. CrowdStrike published its 2026 Threat Hunting Report this morning, with a companion video from CrowdStrike's Katie Blankenship walking through the findings. This is about as easy a re-engagement reason as you'll get all week — call your CrowdStrike accounts, ask if they've seen the new report, and offer to walk their security team through what it means for their environment. From there it's a natural bridge into cross-selling modules tied to the report's findings, or an EDR-to-XDR upgrade conversation if they're not already there.

Fifth and last, a story that applies to nearly every customer you have, not just one vertical. Researchers have tied Russia's Midnight Blizzard group to a campaign stealing Microsoft account credentials through compromised Wi-Fi at hotels and other hospitality venues. Think about how many of your customers have executives or field staff who travel and log into Microsoft accounts from hotel Wi-Fi — that's the exposure here. It's a clean, low-pressure opener for a phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication or Conditional Access conversation, and if you're selling identity products, this is your moment to talk about Entra ID Protection or Zero Trust network access for traveling users.

That's your five for today. One quick pattern worth noticing across all of them: three of these are vendor platforms themselves under active attack — N-able, SonicWall, and indirectly Microsoft credentials through Wi-Fi — which is a good reminder that the tools your customers trust to protect them are just as often the target. Lead with that when you're building urgency on today's calls.
