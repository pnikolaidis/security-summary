Good morning. I'm Allie with your Monday security briefing for June 8th, 2026. Today's digest is heavy on vulnerability disclosures — WordPress, home routers, and enterprise network infrastructure are all in the spotlight — alongside one actively exploited product that needs your immediate attention.

Story one: A wave of critical WordPress vulnerabilities hit the National Vulnerability Database today, and these are not ones to sit on. Five CVEs were published covering multiple WordPress plugins and themes, three of them rated critical at the highest severity level — nine point eight out of ten. The most dangerous are CVE-2023-54352, which affects the Seotheme WordPress theme, and CVE-2024-58348, which hits the Background Image Cropper plugin. Both allow a completely unauthenticated remote attacker to upload arbitrary PHP files to the server and execute them — a full takeover with zero credentials required. A third critical, CVE-2024-58349, affects the Travelscape theme with a similar file-upload flaw. Two more CVEs in the Augmented-Reality plugin and the Sonaar Music Plugin round out the set at high severity. Exploits for all five have been made public, which means opportunistic mass scanning is already likely underway. If you run WordPress, today is the day to audit your installed plugins and themes against these CVE IDs and update or remove affected components.

Story two: Three Tenda home and small-office routers have stack-based buffer overflow vulnerabilities that allow unauthenticated remote attackers to fully compromise the devices. The worst of the three is CVE-2026-11499, rated critical at nine point eight, affecting Tenda HG7, HG9, and HG10 models via the domain-blocking configuration function. CVE-2026-11498 hits the same device family through the VoIP settings interface at a high eight point eight, and CVE-2026-11503 affects the Tenda CX12L through its WiFi setup endpoint, also at eight point eight. All three are remotely exploitable and public exploits are available or expected imminently. Tenda routers are common in home and small-office environments and are notoriously infrequently updated. If you or your organization rely on these models, check for firmware updates immediately, and in the meantime consider restricting access to the web management interface from the WAN side.

Story three: SolarWinds has confirmed that a vulnerability in its Serv-U managed file transfer server is being actively exploited in the wild right now. The flaw allows unauthenticated attackers to crash the Serv-U service with specially crafted HTTP POST requests. SolarWinds has released a patch, but active exploitation means that organizations still running unpatched versions are being targeted today. SolarWinds products are well-known targets — the company has been at the center of major supply-chain incidents in the past — so threat actors are highly motivated to find and exploit weaknesses. If Serv-U is anywhere in your environment, patch it immediately, and review your access logs for unusual POST request activity.

Story four: The Risky Business security bulletin today covers three interconnected stories worth tracking together. First, Cisco has warned of a new zero-day in its SD-WAN product, a critical piece of enterprise networking infrastructure. No patch is yet available, so Cisco SD-WAN administrators should monitor for the official advisory and apply any available mitigations. Second, AT&T and IBM are facing allegations that they concealed foreign hacking incidents from regulators — a disclosure and accountability story that is still developing. And on a more positive note, RubyGems is rolling out dependency cooldown periods to its package registry, a measure designed to slow down supply-chain attacks by preventing immediate automatic updates to newly published package versions. It is the same kind of friction that has helped the npm ecosystem resist certain categories of typosquatting attacks.

Story five: VMware has patched three stored cross-site scripting vulnerabilities in Cloud Foundation Operations — CVE-2026-41722, 41723, and 41724, all rated high at a CVSS of eight. An attacker who already has limited privileges — enough to create policies, views, or text widgets in the platform — can inject persistent JavaScript that executes in other administrators' browsers. In a large VMware environment, that kind of privilege escalation path can lead to full administrative control of virtualization infrastructure. Apply VMware's patch promptly and take a hard look at who in your organization has the ability to create policies and views in Cloud Foundation Operations.

That's your digest for Monday June 8th. The SolarWinds Serv-U active exploitation and the critical WordPress RCEs are the most urgent action items today. Stay safe out there.
