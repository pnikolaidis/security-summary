They just patched a critical bundle of switch and router flaws — seven vulnerabilities in total, four of them rated critical, hitting IOS X R software and the Nexus nine thousand switch line. The standout is a bug that lets an unauthenticated attacker land root access on a Nexus switch remotely, no login required, through a couple of management ports that are reachable by default. Cisco also flagged separate, still-unpatched flaws in Secure Email that could expose encrypted mail content. If you're carrying Cisco networking gear, this is your week to call every Nexus and IOS X R account and confirm they've applied the fix. It's also a solid advisory opener — a quick exposure review to check whether those management ports are reachable from outside — and a good renewal talking point if your team gets them patched fast.

Third, SonicWall is having a rough summer. CISA just added a SonicWall S M A one thousand flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog — a perfect ten severity score, an unauthenticated server-side request forgery bug, and it's actively being used in the wild to drop reverse shells and cryptocurrency miners. This is the third zero-day on SonicWall's edge devices this summer, following two earlier incidents on the same product line. Three zero-days in one season stops looking like bad luck and starts looking like a pattern, and that's a legitimate opening if you're trying to displace SonicWall with a competing edge security platform. It's also worth a quick advisory check-in with existing SonicWall customers on interim mitigations while they get patched.
