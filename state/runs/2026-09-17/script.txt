Aiden Iyer reporting in for Thursday. Today's channel brief, kept tight — five things worth raising on calls today.

First up, and this is the one to lead with: Cisco just pushed an emergency, out of band patch for a maximum severity Identity Services Engine vulnerability that attackers are already exploiting in the wild. It's unauthenticated, remotely exploitable, and lets an attacker bypass authentication entirely with a crafted request. It landed alongside a broader batch of critical fixes across ISE, FMC, and Nexus Dashboard covering everything from command injection to SQL injection to full root access. Six critical CVEs in total. If you're carrying Cisco identity or network gear, this is your reason to call every ISE customer today and ask if they've patched yet. It's also an advisory opener — offer a patch verification and posture review — and if you compete with Cisco, it's a clean displacement angle into Zscaler, Okta, or CyberArk conversations about moving identity off an appliance with this kind of exposure.

Second, a pipeline trigger out of the public sector. The ShinyHunters extortion gang breached Florida's motor vehicle database and published thousands of drivers' records online after the state agency refused to pay the ransom. The data went public anyway, which is exactly the outcome every ransomware negotiation training warns about. This is a great one to bring to any state or local government prospect, or frankly any customer sitting on a lot of citizen personal data — it's a real, recent example of what happens when you say no to an extortion demand without the incident response and communications playbook to back it up. Good advisory opener on ransomware readiness and negotiation posture, and a good pentest opener too — better to find that kind of exposure yourself before the next extortion crew does.

Third, mobile. Google confirmed a bug in the Pixel modem that may be under limited, targeted exploitation, and says some Pixel phone owners have already been hacked in zero-day attacks. Details on scope are still thin, but this is exactly the kind of headline mobile security customers are going to ask about directly, so get ahead of it. It's a solid excuse to bring up mobile threat defense on your next call — ask whether their current stack would even catch something happening down at the baseband or modem layer, because most endpoint tools won't. Good expansion opener if you're not already attached to a customer's mobile fleet.

Fourth, and this one matters for anyone selling into AI forward engineering teams. An attacker hijacked a live AI coding assistant session at an unnamed software as a service company, and the assistant recommended a poisoned package that the developer accepted. That single accepted recommendation let the Shai-Hulud worm spread across roughly a hundred internal repositories, stealing repository secrets and source code as it went. Every customer rolling out AI coding assistants inside their development pipeline needs to hear this story. It's a clean advisory opener — a focused security review of their AI dev tooling and software supply chain controls — and honestly a good pentest pitch too, since red-teaming the AI tooling itself is still a novel ask most competitors aren't making yet.

Fifth and last, a vertical specific stat worth keeping in your back pocket. New research shows ransomware attacks on manufacturers are up forty percent so far this year, as attackers increasingly exploit the operational shutdown pressure a hit manufacturer faces to force a faster payout. If you've got manufacturing accounts on your list, lead with this number on your next call. It supports an OT and IT posture assessment conversation, it's a strong pentest engagement opener, and it's a good excuse to cross-sell into any account with meaningful supply chain exposure.

That's your brief. Aiden out — go close something.
